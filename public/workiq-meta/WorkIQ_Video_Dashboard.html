        Two lenses, on purpose: All time = per video FILE via Meta's video_asset breakdown (the only way to compare individual videos/crops) — it undercounts because Meta doesn't attribute flexible/Advantage+ creative spend to specific assets, so it will not match Ads Manager. Last 90 / Last 10 days = AD level, which does match Ads Manager but can't separate individual video files inside a flexible ad. Ads are tagged by decoding the naming convention (Format | Style | Talent | Hook | Action | Date | V#). 69 videos under $500 lifetime spend are excluded as noise (only $5,441 combined — vs $379,109 shown); any video with spend in the last 10 days is always included, so the Last-10-days view is complete. Reconciling with Ads Manager: this dashboard covers video assets only — static images, carousels, and boosted posts are excluded, so account-level Ads Manager totals will be higher. Purchases follow the account's attribution setting and continue to update for ~7 days after an impression, so a very recent window will read lower here than Ads Manager does later. Verdicts — ad rows (recent): Scale ROAS ≥ target · Keep 0.9–target · Cut? < 0.9 · Low signal < 5 purchases. Video rows (all time): Live · strong/ok/weak = still spending in the last 90 days · Revive? = retired with lifetime ROAS ≥ target+0.4 and ≥ 15 purchases · Retired = no recent spend. Monthly trend: bars = monthly spend (height) colored by that month's ROAS; ▲/▼ = recent-month ROAS ≥15% above/below the video's earlier months (fatigue signal), shown only when there's enough purchase volume to mean something. Days = distinct days with spend from daily-level pulls, never the calendar span. Video rows show lifetime days; ad rows show days inside the selected window, with lifetime days after the slash (e.g. "10d / 36" = spent on all 10 days of this window, 36 days since it launched). First spend = the first day that ad ever recorded spend (daily data covers 2026 onward). Ad set is shown and filterable in the recent (ad-level) views — the same creative often runs in several ad sets, and that's usually what explains different results for the same video. Seasonality caveat: Q4 runs (gifting) tend to inflate ROAS vs summer — compare like-for-like periods when possible.
